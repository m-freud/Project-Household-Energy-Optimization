
# 2. System design

The whole setup is mostly unchanged from the [original dataset](/data/dataset_original/paper.pdf):

Each household has a base load and two electric vehicles. 200 households get a PV system, 150 thereof a battery unit (BESS)

One notable change:
EV2 is now only used privately. So instead of one predictable commute to and from work, like EV1, EV2 can have an arbitrary number of trips without access to a charging station.


### Household
-> [class Household](/src/simulation/household.py)

Each simulation run takes place in a household object.
It stores the household id, active scenario, current timestep,
and household-level inputs like base load and electricity prices.

It also tracks control actions and the history of the combined system.

PV, BESS and EVs are separate device models attached to the household.

The household currently does not model resident data like demograhics or behaviour.

### EV1
EV1 is used for exactly one round trip to and from work, using up battery energy both ways.

It can be charged at home or at work, where there is a charging station available.
Home charging prices vary throughout the day, while station prices are fixed.

Work commutes take place during fixed windows:

To work: between 8am and 12:30pm
Back home: between 17:30pm and 22:00 pm

### EV2
EV2 is used for an arbitrary number of commutes that are not work-related. 

It can only be charged at home.

Trip times and durations are directly sourced from real-world consumer data from Australia.


### PV
200 out of 250 households get a PV system that generates electricity directly available to the household grid, effectively lowering the total load.


PV generation data is sourced from real-world consumer data in southern Germany.


### BESS
150 out of the 200 PV households have an energy storage system (BESS) that can be used to store energy and release it back into the household whenever needed.

It provides a direct way of storing energy fo later usage.

### Energy flows
Household demand is supplied by the grid, the PV system, and the battery.
The battery can store energy and release it later, with losses during charging and discharging.
Surplus PV can be fed into the battery or sold to the grid.
EVs are charged from the battery, grid, or station if at work (EV1 only).

Currently we do not implement E2H or E2G, although we might in the future.
